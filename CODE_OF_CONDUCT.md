# Code of Conduct

DataBug is a project anyone can use and improve. We want taking part to be welcoming and harassment-free for everyone, whatever their experience, background, identity or the databases they work with.

This applies to everywhere the project happens: issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, release notes and any other space run for DataBug. It also applies when you represent the project elsewhere.

## Expected behaviour

- Be kind and patient, especially with newcomers and people asking questions.
- Assume good intent. Ask before concluding that someone meant harm.
- Give feedback about the work, not the person. Explain the *why* behind a request for changes.
- Accept feedback gracefully. Disagreement is fine; keep it focused on what's best for the project and its users.
- Credit other people's work and ideas.
- Respect people's time: keep discussions on topic and say clearly when you can't follow through on something.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, intimidation, threats or stalking, in public or in private
- Insults, slurs, or demeaning comments about anyone's identity, appearance or abilities
- Sexual language or imagery, or unwelcome attention of any kind
- Trolling, deliberately derailing discussions, or repeated off-topic posting
- Sharing someone's private information (including connection details, credentials or data from their databases) without their explicit permission
- Pressuring people to keep working on something after they've said no
- Encouraging any of the above

## Reporting

If someone's behaviour concerns you, contact the maintainer privately: [@hayes-roach on GitHub](https://github.com/hayes-roach). For anything sensitive, use the repository's private reporting form (**Security → Report a vulnerability**) and say in the title that it's a conduct report; only maintainers can read it.

Reports are kept confidential. You won't face any consequences for reporting in good faith, even if no action ends up being taken.

## Enforcement

Maintainers will review every report and respond as quickly as they can. Depending on what happened, they may:

1. **Correct:** a private note explaining what was wrong, sometimes with a request for a public apology.
2. **Warn:** a warning with consequences for repeating the behaviour, and a pause in interaction with the people involved.
3. **Temporary ban:** no participation in the project for a set period.
4. **Permanent ban:** for serious or repeated violations, including sustained harassment.

Maintainers may remove, edit or reject comments, commits, code, issues and other contributions that break this code. Maintainers who don't follow it in good faith may be removed from their role.
